Option A - Safety across the top

Open full-size option A

A broad safety panel leads, with the seven conditions arranged below in two open rows.

Preparing option A. If this message remains, serve this page over HTTP and enable JavaScript to review the proposal.
Proposed speaker notes

Lead with both kinds of safety and give them time. Leadership needed operational safety: no downtime, support unaffected and a team able to adopt the new way of working. Answer the question of whether this could take the estate down before starting. Engineers also needed psychological safety: room to learn while protecting the estate, with mistakes treated as learning rather than blame. All seven conditions depend on both kinds of safety.

Make psychological safety explicit, out loud and more than once. Do not assume it exists because the team seems friendly. It has to continue when somebody breaks something that matters.

Take the seven conditions in turn. The numbers identify the conditions and do not impose a strict sequence. Get non-technical stakeholders and senior leadership bought in on the priority before starting. Ours were slow to understand it, and that cost early momentum.

Gate workshop attendance on completed pre-requisite learning, with no exceptions. Engineers who skipped the pre-work held back their whole cohort. Keep days rather than weeks between learning something and practising it for real, because long gaps caused rapid knowledge loss.

Use remote learning for self-paced pre-requisites. Pairing and labs worked far better face to face. Build the relationship with whoever owns Terraform and GitHub early, because their help with access, guardrails and troubleshooting is needed throughout.

Set expectations for the dip up front. Our team slowed down before getting faster. It took two to three months before people were comfortable raising and reviewing pull requests. That duration is confirmed. Setting the expectation early helps people understand the initial friction as part of learning.

Choose the people who will approve pull requests early and explicitly train them to review. Nobody on the team had used Terraform or GitHub at the start. Someone needs to be qualified to gate a change when the work is handed over, so approval ownership cannot wait until the week before handover.
