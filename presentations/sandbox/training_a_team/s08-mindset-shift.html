Where the content comes from

Two sources, deliberately kept apart. The shape of the list is what the research says is hardest about picking up Terraform and GitOps: the move from imperative to declarative ("I tell it what to do" to "I describe what should be true") is the single most reported stumbling block for people coming from scripts and consoles; state and drift are the next; and the culture change is reported far more often than the tooling as the thing that stalls GitOps adoption, with the CNCF survey putting cultural challenges ahead of technical ones. The words are all yours: every shift below is anchored in a line this deck already carries, so nothing here is invented.

  • The console is where I work → the repo is where I work. Slide 3: "Every change to production goes through a pull request. Zero manual console changes... the answer is always check the commit history, never ask around."

  • I tell it what to do → I describe what should be true. Slide 3's "read a plan and predict exactly what will change before approving it", and phase 2 of the pathway on slide 9.

  • I fix it now → I raise it, and someone reviews it. Slide 3: "make it, get it reviewed, ship it." Condition 07 on slide 7: deciding who is qualified to approve.

  • I am the expert here → I am a beginner again. Slide 5, verbatim: "Prior coding experience predicted success, not Jamf Pro familiarity or age." The most uncomfortable line in the deck and the one that most deserves a slide.

  • A mistake is a risk → a mistake is recoverable. Slide 3: "Because mistakes are recoverable, engineers make changes they would previously have avoided or escalated." Slide 7 on psychological safety, slide 6 on safe labs.

  • This will make me faster → this makes me slower, first. Condition 06 on slide 7: "Expect the dip... two to three months before people were comfortable raising and reviewing PRs."

The closing line on options A, B and D - "None of these are Terraform skills. They are what had to be true before the Terraform skills stuck." - is the one piece of new writing rather than a restatement. It is an editorial claim, not a fact about the estate, so cut it if you disagree with it.
