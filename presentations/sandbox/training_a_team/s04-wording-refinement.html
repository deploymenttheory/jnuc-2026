Proposed speaker notes

Start with the secondment, then take the five points in order. Use the arrows to close on what each team contributed.

We seconded one Mac engineer into the DevOps Centre of Excellence full time for three months on a train-the-trainer model. Three months describes the secondment only. We ran the training iteratively, rather than fixing the whole programme upfront. This was a posting, not a course or a shadowing arrangement.

We wrote the training materials together. The CoE brought the Terraform and Git content, and the seconded engineer put it in Mac language and cut what did not apply. The arrows show the two-way exchange: macOS and Jamf Pro domain knowledge went into the CoE, and Terraform, Git and GitOps practice came back to the Mac team.

We went back and forth between theory-led and hands-on delivery. Practical sessions landed better, so that is where the format settled. The hackathons did not work as intended. They used in-person labs on tasks the team already recognised, with homework between sessions. Keep the positive result for practical delivery separate from the hackathon result.

We had no existing playbook to copy for teaching Terraform to our Mac team, so we worked the plan out as we went. Describe our own starting point here, without claiming that nobody elsewhere had done similar training.

We used different plans by skill level. Higher-skill engineers had a more laid-back, project-based plan. Others followed a more structured plan matched to where they were starting from.

The secondment also gave us someone who could step in and run migration work whenever needed. Keep this as a supporting consequence of combining the two sets of knowledge, with training the team as the purpose of the secondment.
