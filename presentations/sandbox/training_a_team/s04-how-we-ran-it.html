Option A - The secondment

Open full-size option A

Put the decision at the centre: one Mac engineer, full time in the CoE for three months. Pick this for the clearest introduction to how the programme began, with the exchange of knowledge as the supporting visual.

Proposed speaker notes for A

Lead with the large duration, then connect it to the engineer and the purpose. The two lines below show what each side contributed. Keep the three months attached to the secondment.

We seconded one Mac engineer into the DevOps Centre of Excellence full time for three months on a train-the-trainer model. That was the length of the secondment. We ran the programme iteratively rather than fixing the whole plan upfront.

We wrote the materials together. The CoE brought the Terraform and Git content, while the seconded engineer translated it into Mac language and cut what did not apply. Knowledge moved both ways: macOS and Jamf Pro domain context went into the CoE, and Terraform, Git and GitOps practice came back to the Mac team.

We went back and forth between theory-led and hands-on delivery. Practical sessions landed better and became the settled format. The hackathons did not work as intended. They were in-person labs on familiar tasks, with homework between sessions. That particular format failing sits alongside the finding that practical delivery worked better overall.

We had no playbook to copy for this Mac-centric Terraform migration training, so we worked out the plan as we went. We matched delivery to prior skill: higher-skill engineers had a more laid-back, project-based plan, while others had a more structured plan suited to their starting point.

The secondment also left us with someone who could step in and run migration work whenever needed. Keep that as a supporting consequence of the knowledge exchange, with training the team as the purpose of the secondment.
