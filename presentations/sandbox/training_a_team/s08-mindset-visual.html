Mindset shift - visual round

Round two. The content is settled and unchanged: the same six pairs, the same old mindset to new mindset device, the same closing line, all still anchored in lines the deck already carries. Round one's four layouts were all built out of the deck's existing vocabulary - grids, hairlines, bordered cards - which is exactly why none of them stood out. Every option below breaks that vocabulary somewhere, and each one breaks it differently: A changes the colour of the ground, B changes the shape of the slide, C changes what a pair is, D takes everything away except the type.

Option A - Inverted stage

The one slide in the deck that is not on the royal blue ground. The canvas turns lime and the ink turns navy, and the six pairs sit on it in the same two by three grid. It stands out because it is the only time the deck changes colour, which is the cheapest and loudest signal available and reads instantly even from the back. It costs a little plumbing that ships with it: the footer hairline moves to the deepened lime, and the white JNUC mark takes the navy plate the themed presets already give it. The counter and the progress bar are page-level chrome rather than slide content, so they stay as they are and the lime progress bar disappears into the lime ground for this one slide - tell me if that matters and it can be recoloured. Worth knowing it also inverts in every one of the seven themes, so it is worth a look in light-paper before you commit.

Option B - Two grounds, one hard edge

    
Splits the slide down the middle with a full-bleed navy panel: the old mindset lives on the panel, the new one on the canvas, and the title moves onto the panel with it. The shape is the argument, and nothing else in this deck is built asymmetrically, so it reads as its own thing without changing a single colour. The trade is the same one option B made last time - two full columns leave no room for the consequence lines, so those move into your narration. It is also the only option whose wrapper has to escape the slide padding to reach the edge, which is a slightly unusual thing to have in the deck.

Option C - Turn cards

    
Six cards, each one visibly turned over: the old belief on the navy top, the new one on a lime half cut in on the diagonal. Every pair becomes a single object with the turn inside it rather than two lines of text sitting near each other, and the six lime wedges give the slide a texture nothing else in the deck has. Densest of the four and the most decorative, so it is the one most likely to look like a graphic exercise if the room is not in the mood for it. The consequence lines come off to keep the cards breathing.

Option D - Ghost type

    
No cards, no rules, no second ground. The belief being put down is set at exactly the same size and weight as the one replacing it and then faded back to a quarter strength, so each pair reads as one sentence being overwritten by another. It stands out by being the only slide in the deck with nothing on it but type at scale, which after eleven slides of bordered cards is its own kind of loud. The quietest of the four and the one that depends most on the words being right - which, since you have already signed those off, may be exactly the point.
