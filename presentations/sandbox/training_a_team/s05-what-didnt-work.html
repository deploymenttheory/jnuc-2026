Proposed speaker notes

Take time over this slide because it is useful to anyone planning a training programme. Explain the chart as six teaching sessions across six months. Each session raised confidence and the following gap gave much of that gain back. The floor still rose after every cycle, so people genuinely built confidence over the programme. The shape is illustrative, not measured. The six months, six sessions and 144 hours are real.

The 144 hours amounted to roughly four weeks of learning, which was about right for the number of topics. The difficulty was spreading that learning across six months. People learnt a little, stopped and came back later. Interest wandered between sessions and some of the confidence gained in the previous session faded during the gap. Distinguish the amount of learning from the much longer calendar period.

Mixed ability meant that trainers pitched some sessions too fast and others too slowly for parts of the room. Prior coding experience predicted success more than Jamf Pro familiarity or age, which surprised us. Teaching the theory before people had practical experience left them without a mental model, so much of what they heard went in one ear and out the other.

The first curriculum contained 214,645 words of AI-assisted material. We wrote it for completeness, like a reference manual, and it was too large for people to take in. Step-by-step tutorials became copy-paste exercises. People completed them without retaining what they had done.

The main reason for the long gaps was the difficulty of gathering the Mac engineering team in one room. Team members worked in different countries. Unclear infrastructure availability made scheduling a little worse in a few cases, but it was a minor factor. Keep geography as the main explanation.

In-person delivery worked better than online meetings. Trainers could walk around the room and help people directly as soon as they got stuck. On a call, people tended to mute themselves and struggle alone. The engineer's feedback captures the problem: I understood the slides but had no idea what to actually do when I opened VS Code.

The next slide explains what replaced the original material: 3,778 words, 57 times smaller. We wrote it after direct feedback that the first version was too heavy going and too theoretical. Keep that replacement on the next slide.
