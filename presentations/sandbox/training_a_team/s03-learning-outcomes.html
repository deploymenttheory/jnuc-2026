Option A - The finish line

Proposed speaker notes for A

Start with the finish line. An engineer counted as trained when they could take a real change from branch to production unaided. Pause on that word, while making clear that the normal process still includes peer review. The three statements underneath are the behaviours we actually observed.

We wanted any team member to be able to pick up any change and take it through review into production with confidence. Collaboration meant externalising domain knowledge from people's heads into self-documenting code, reducing single points of failure and catching mistakes before production. Every engineer needed to read a terraform plan and predict its effect before approval, and to recognise when breakglass GUI access or stopping to ask for review was the right call.

Fast recovery meant returning to a known good configuration. Once mistakes were recoverable, engineers took on changes they would previously have avoided or escalated. Normal production changes go through a pull request, with the commit history explaining what changed, when and why for stakeholders and external audit. Breakglass GUI write access remains the exception to that normal route.

The observed results were that the Mac engineering team became self-sufficient committing and reviewing pull requests without assistance, they were making architectural decisions, and resources were being managed in code exclusively. These are the reported outcomes, distinct from the benefits we intended the programme to bring.

For individuals, the intended benefits were transferable skills, recoverable mistakes and lower cognitive load across instances. For the team, they were fewer single points of failure, shared engineering practices and language with cloud teams, awareness of changes across teams, learning rather than blame, collaboration without conflicting changes, and Terraform modules that sister engineering teams could consume through self-service. For the organisation, the aims were compliance and audit without a scramble, governance without slowing delivery, and deployment guardrails that scale beyond one team.

An engineer counted as trained when they could take a real change from branch to production unaided. That finish line sits alongside the required peer review in the normal change process. The goals were not to make everyone a Terraform expert, promise zero incidents or abandon Jamf GUI knowledge; that knowledge still matters for breakglass.
