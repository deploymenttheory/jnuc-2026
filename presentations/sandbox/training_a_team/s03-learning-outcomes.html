Proposed speaker notes for A

Read the numbered outcomes as seven related capabilities, then define the finish line at the foot. Their numbering is a speaking order, not a ranked list.

These are the capabilities we wanted every engineer to develop. Learning outcomes sit here; the benefits for individuals, teams and the organisation, alongside what success looked like, now have their own slide later in the talk.

Collaboration means working on changes together and externalising domain knowledge from people's heads into self-documenting code. That reduces single points of failure and gives the team a chance to catch mistakes before they reach production. Team confidence means being able to implement changes with GitOps and Terraform and perform your role effectively: any team member can pick up a change, make it, get it reviewed and ship it.

Fast recovery means the team can revert to a known good configuration when something goes wrong. Recoverability also gives engineers the confidence to attempt changes they would previously have avoided or escalated. Every engineer should be able to read a Terraform plan and predict what will change before approving it. Every safe apply depends on that skill.

Judgement matters as much as using the tools. Engineers need to recognise when breakglass GUI access is the right call and when to stop and ask for review. Normal changes to production go through a pull request, with commit history providing the source of truth about how Jamf Pro reached its current state. Breakglass GUI write access is an exception, consistent with the operational model described on slide 2.

Engineers should be able to show stakeholders or external audit what changed, when and why on demand. Traceability belongs in the workflow, rather than relying on people to reconstruct a change from memory.

Name the finish line: an engineer counted as trained when they could take a real change from branch to production unaided. Unaided means they did not need a trainer to carry them through it; normal peer review and approval still apply. Be honest about the non-goals: not everyone becomes a Terraform expert, incidents still happen, and Jamf GUI knowledge still matters for breakglass.
