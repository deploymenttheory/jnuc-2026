Proposed speaker notes for A

Start with what the learner stands to gain, then follow the benefits through the team to the organisation. These were intended benefits of the training and way of working, rather than a measured list of outcomes.

For individuals, the aim was new transferable engineering skills, a safety net that makes mistakes recoverable, and less cognitive load because the same changes no longer have to be repeated across different instances.

For the team, we wanted knowledge to be shared so there was no single point of failure. The team would use the same engineering practices and language as cloud teams, have awareness of changes across teams, treat failures as learning opportunities without blame, and collaborate without conflicting changes. Terraform modules would also let sister engineering teams consume configuration through self service.

For the organisation, the intended benefits were compliance and audit without a last-minute scramble, governance without slowing delivery, and deployment guardrails that scale beyond one team.

Keep the observed behaviour separate from those intended benefits. The Mac engineering team became self-sufficient committing and reviewing pull requests without assistance, they were making architectural decisions, and resources were being managed exclusively in code. Normal changes went through code; breakglass GUI access remained the exception described elsewhere in the talk.
