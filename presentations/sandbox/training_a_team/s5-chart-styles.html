Slide 5 - What didn't work, the hero chart

The line-and-gradient chart at the top of the slide was rejected, so all three options below are new drawings and none of them is the version that was live before this PR. The story is identical in all three and is not up for discussion: six months, six teaching sessions, 144 hours, a real dip after every gap, and a gentle net climb overall. Only the visual language changes. The chips, the quote strip and the "SHAPE IS ILLUSTRATIVE" label are the same in every render. All three are already in the deck as markup wrappers inside .chartwrap; option A is what the deck shows today and each render below only flips which wrapper is visible.

ABC

Option A - Gain and give-back columns in the deck now

One column per month instead of a continuous line. The solid lime block is the confidence the team still had at the end of the month; the dashed outline sitting on top of it is what that month's teaching had reached before the following gap took it back. A dashed trend line joins the tops of the solid blocks so the net climb reads at a glance, and a strip under the axis shows the teaching session and then the long dotted gap that follows it, six times. Pick this if you want the "we kept losing part of every session" point to be countable rather than felt: the give-back is a discrete quantity in the same units as the gain, and the columns are the easiest of the three to read from the back of the room.

Option B - The ratchet

    
Hard straight lines only, no curves and no gradient. Each teaching session is a short steep lime riser inside its shaded band; each gap is a longer, shallower coral fall. Faint dotted floor lines run right from each post-gap low point, so the eye can see that every floor sits above the one before it. Pick this if the mechanism is the message: the shape says plainly that progress was made in bursts and given back slowly, and that the programme worked in spite of the calendar rather than because of it. It is the most diagrammatic of the three and the least like a business chart.

Option C - Blocks per month

    
No axes at all. Six rows, one per month, each a run of small blocks reading left to right: dim blocks carried in from the month before, solid lime blocks gained in that month's teaching session, and dashed outlined blocks at the tip that the following gap took back off the end. The rows get longer down the stack, so the six months, the six sessions and the six dips are all countable at once, and the accumulation is a shape rather than a slope. Pick this if you want the audience to be able to point at a specific loss ("three blocks, every single month") while still seeing the total grow; it is also the calmest of the three and sits comfortably above the chips.
