Slide 21 - links reformat and QR placeholder

Joseph asked to reformat the links slide to look better and to add a placeholder for the QR code the training deck carries on its own resources slide - the QR should have come across with the links in PR #85 and did not. The QR's final target URL is not settled, so every option below reserves the space in the training deck's QR visual style (a white tile, the deck's own --c-photo token, matching .qr-tile in training_a_team/index.html) with a TODO: QR target URL chip standing in for the code itself, never a fake scannable pattern. All eight links, both group headings, the outstanding jamfpy/contact/socials chip and the QR wording ("Take it with you" / "Scan for a link to this presentation") are identical across all three - only the layout changes.

Each option is a real markup wrapper inside #s18 with its own CSS already in the deck, so the injected rule below only flips which wrapper is visible - and accepting one means deleting the other two wrappers and their rules as a single block.

The two link columns stay side by side, narrowed a little to make room for a third column: a quiet QR rail carrying the "Take it with you" label, the reserved tile and the caption, echoing the training deck's own three-column resources slide. Option B - one footer bar for what's still to come

    
The two link columns are untouched - same grid, same width, same order. Everything unresolved about the slide moves into one horizontal bar below a hairline rule: a small QR tile on the left, the "Take it with you" wording beside it, and the outstanding chip pinned to the right of the same row. Where option A treats the QR as a third column of equal standing, this option treats the QR and the outstanding chip as one unit - a "what's still to come" strip - and keeps the links themselves exactly as they render today. Pick this if the links should look unchanged and the QR should read as a footnote rather than a feature.

Option C - narrower columns, a QR hero card

    
The two link columns shrink to make room for a much bigger QR panel on the right: a bordered navy card, the same surface the deck already uses for its other panels, holding the label, a tile roughly the size of the training deck's own QR code, the caption and the outstanding chip all together.
