Slide 2 - column titles

Joseph's feedback merged three pairs of constraint cards down to one shared source of truth for secrets/linters and for peer review/single source, and dropped the tenant tidy-up line, which frees one cell per column. This PR ships that consolidation and turns the three freed cells into column titles - Context, Requirements, Constraints - reading Context: the merged secrets/linters card and the CLI-in-dev-only card; Requirements: the merged peer review/shared source card and the versioned releases card; Constraints: the strategic tooling card and the Terraform-only card, unchanged. The consolidation and the six cards are identical in all three renders below; only the title treatment for the freed cell changes.

ABC

Option A - Plain heading live now

The title sits as plain heading text in the freed cell - small caps, muted, monospace, in the deck's existing group-title style. The two cards below sit unchanged. This is the lightest touch: it reads as a label over each column rather than a shape in the grid, so the three-by-three card rhythm is broken deliberately in favour of a quieter, more editorial page. Pick this if the column grouping should read as organisation rather than as decoration - the titles guide the eye without adding visual weight.

Option B - Filled title tile

    
The title becomes a filled tile the same size and shape as the cards beneath it - accent background, ink text, same radius and padding - so the freed cell keeps the exact shape of a card and the grid still reads as nine equal-sized tiles, just with the top row recoloured. Pick this if the three-by-three rhythm of the original grid mattered more than the fact that a cell changed meaning - it keeps the page visually identical in structure to how it always looked, with colour doing the work of marking the shift from label to content.

Option C - Bordered column panel

    
The card treatment moves from the individual items to the column itself: each column becomes one bordered panel with the title as its header and the two cards as plain rows, separated by a hairline, inside it. The three panels share their row heights, so the hairline sits at the same height in every block (amended 2026-08-27 at Joseph's request). Pick this if the grouping into three columns is the thing that most needs to read clearly - the panel border does the grouping work that whitespace alone does in A and B, so it is the strongest signal that "these two facts belong together under this heading", at the cost of losing the original nine-card texture.
